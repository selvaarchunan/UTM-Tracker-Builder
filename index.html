What each UTM means

utm_source

Who sent the traffic. Examples: google, linkedin, newsletter, from_blog.

utm_medium

How the link was distributed. Examples: cpc, email, referral, paid_social, qr.

utm_campaign

The campaign that the visit belongs to. Keep one consistent name across all campaign links.

utm_content

Separates links or creative versions, such as blog_link, kochi, header_button or banner_a.

utm_term

Optional. Commonly used to identify a paid search keyword or targeting term.

Campaign ID

Optional internal identifier. In the URL it is added as utm_id.

Good practice: use lowercase, avoid spaces, and keep spelling consistent. For example, use spring_launch everywhere rather than switching between several versions of the campaign name.

How to use your UTM link

  1. Enter the exact page you want visitors to open.
  2. Fill in the source, medium and campaign. Add content when you need to compare cities, placements or creative versions.
  3. Copy the generated URL and use it in the relevant blog, social post, QR code, email or advertisement.
  4. In GA4, review Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition and use session source/medium or session campaign to assess traffic.
  5. Track completed registrations as a GA4 key event. UTMs identify traffic sources; they do not measure successful registrations by themselves.
Important: UTMs are normally read when a new session begins. Use tagged links on external campaigns, not internal links between pages on your own website, because internal UTMs can overwrite attribution.
